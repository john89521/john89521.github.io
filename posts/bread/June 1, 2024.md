# 9 Bread Baking Tools Every Bread Baker Should Have

If you are going to start making sourdough bread at home consistently, then you need to possess good baking tools. Here is my list of tools I feel are absolutely essential.  

## Big Bowls  
For the beginning bread baker, a large bowl will do just fine. On average, your loaf will have 3 cups of flour so a large bowl should handle that amount just fine. The bowl can be aluminum or ceramic or even plastic, but it needs to be sturdy. I like to have several, at least two, because I often will be making several loaves at a time, for example one regular and another rye.  

## Danish Dough Whisk  
You really have to trust me on this one. A Danish dough whisk is a fantastic tool. For some reason, this thing mixes dough perfectly, better than a large spoon or spatula. You want to get your dough mixed enough to handle. This tool does that quickly and efficiently. And here is the good part: the Danish dough whisk is inexpensive. I paid $10 for two on Amazon.  

## Silicone Oven Mitt or Pot Holders  
This is another tool that is absolutely essential. You will be pulling very hot pans out of the oven and handling very hot bread. If you are like me, you will be doing this practically daily. You want a tool that allows you to quickly reach in the oven and pull these things out. You will be moving very hot loaves to the cooling rack, so you might as well not worry about burning your hands. There are several different types on the market and come in all shapes and sizes and price ranges. Just a pair so you can have some peace of mind when working with extremely hot items.  

## Bread Knife  
You are going to be cutting a lot of bread so get yourself a high quality blade. This is one where I say you spend the money to get a good, quality knife. I use one with a good serrated edge and it cuts through all types of bread like a Luke Skywalker lightsaber. It is simply a joy not to have to struggle through a loaf of crusty bread.  

Side note here. You see a lot of bakers slit their bread with a razor, and that is great for making designs on their bread. However, a good bread knife can also get the job done when slitting the dough before it goes into the oven. That’s my $0.02.  

## Bread Pans or Baking Stone  
Those good old fashion bread baking pans are still good to use with your fancy sourdough bread. Your bread doesn’t have to come out in a perfectly round, perfectly scored circle. If you want to go for that, then have at it. But I have seen some gorgeous loaves of sourdough bread come from rectangle bread pans. Check out joseybakerbread on Instagram.  

As far as baking stones, I love mine. It is multipurpose in the way that I can bake several loaves at once (all at a medium size - 14" x 16" x 1/2" thick) or I can bake a pizza on it.  

## Cutting Board  
I recommend a good sized cutting board. Now, any cutting board will do, but the larger the better. When you have a large cutting board, it suddenly is able to perform double duty. You can now knead the bread much easier on a larger surface and that will help as you start to make larger loaves. Also, a large cutting board is great when you have to carve a 20 pound Thanksgiving turkey. Now, if you have granite countertops, well, you are lucky and this whole business of a large cutting board can really be ignored.  

## Butter  
I’m talking about really good, high grade, expensive butter. You are going to be making a lot of bread, and yes you will eat it without butter to gage the sourness of the bread; but you will also want to put some butter on it to mix up the eating experience. Plus, some family and friends will just want butter on their bread, so you might as well heighten the homemade bread experience and get really good butter. Remember, you have to let that bread cool and that is very difficult, but when it cools just enough, you will be cutting into warm, fresh bread. Put some quality butter on that slice and you will experience one of life’s purest joys. And when you toast it just right, and then put butter on it, your taste buds do the happy dance and joy spread throughout your body.  

## Olive Oil  
Buy some high grade olive oil because I recommend dipping your homemade sourdough masterpiece in the stuff. There are shops around my town that specialize in olive oil, plus they have high grade balsamic vinegar. Since you can get everything off the internet, Google around if you do not have a specialty store nearby. Of course, you can always trust Williams Sonoma.  

## Steam Making Device (Atomizer)  
You will want to get steam into that oven right after the loaves are put in. You can use a simple spray bottle and squirt a mist in. One of the more clever items I have seen use in bread baking is a pump spray bottle - that gets a lot of moisture into that oven. Another clever item I have seen is a small cast iron pan (real small) and then water poured into it (because it was placed in the oven when the oven heated up). I use a tortilla grill - place it in when I turn on the oven then pour some water on it after the loaves are set in.  

### The following items should be considered “nice to haves”. You can bake delicious bread just fine without them, but they will enhance your baking experience. Frankly, I say you should treat yourself and buy the following items.  

#### KitchenAid Mixer  
It is okay not to knead your bread by hand, especially when you start churning out a lot of loaves. The purest say knead the bread by hand so you can feel the texture. This is true - you will be able to tell how sticky or dry it is as you knead it. But what about when the recipe calls for 100% hydration and that does is running in a couple different directions? I find it easier to put in a mixer and let it handle the wet dough.  


#### Dutch Oven  
A lot of bakers use dutch ovens and I do recommend having one in your cupboard. However, if you are baking several loaves at a time, then you should look into purchasing a baking stone. But for one loaf, a dutch oven is superb.  

#### Romertopf Clay Cooker  
The thinking behind this is exactly like the dutch oven. I use a clay cooker because I have always used a clay cooker. I get excellent results from the clay cooker. I bake with the lid on for 20 to 30 minutes, then take the lid off for the remainder of the bake for a brown, crusty loaf.  

#### Baskets/Banneton  
These come in round or long form and are very nice to have because the more you have the more bread you can make. And they leave those nice flour lines on your bread, thus making your sourdough loaf quite pleasing to the eye.  

Okay, you now have a lot of information. I’ll end it there and you go bake some bread.
